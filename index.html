01 Problem & Context

The illusion of the snapshot

Mechanically ventilated ICU patients carry the highest mortality risk in the hospital yet current tools assess them with cross-sectional snapshots, not continuous trajectories.

  • Static scoring systems APACHE II, SOFA and SAPS emit a single admission score; they never reflect how the patient is changing.
  • Irregular sampling vitals every 15–60 min, ventilator settings vary, labs every 6–24 h, medications event-based.
  • Outdated values last-observation-carried-forward keeps hour-old labs “alive” with no discount on reliability.
  • Siloed monitoring hemodynamics, ventilation, labs and medications reviewed separately; subtle multi-variable drift is missed.
Result: delayed recognition of deterioration and missed windows for early intervention.

02 Why It Matters

The clinical cost of invisible data

30–60%+

High stakes

In-hospital mortality in mechanically ventilated patients ranges from 30% to over 60% depending on the underlying etiology.

Human limits

Deterioration is often gradual across many parameters between 2:00 and 6:00 AM when staffing and vigilance are lowest. Watching 15+ variables around the clock exceeds human cognitive load.

The ultimate goal

A 4–6 hour head start can mean the difference between reversible deterioration and catastrophic, refractory collapse.

Example: slowly rising FiO₂ + falling MAP + rising lactate no single alarm fires, yet the combined trajectory pattern predicts imminent mortality risk hours before collapse.

04 Proposed Solution

Continuous risk intelligence

A dynamic, near-continuous risk intelligence system for mechanically ventilated adult ICU patients.

  • Continuous ingestion hemodynamics, ventilator parameters, labs, medications, fluid balance.
  • 24-hour mortality prediction refreshed at regular intervals as new data are charted.
  • Stale-variable mechanism per-parameter time-since-measurement discounts outdated values.
  • Trajectory & escalation-velocity features rates of change and cumulative trends capture deterioration dynamics
  • Explainable alertson the Prototype dashboard “rising FiO₂, falling MAP, vasopressor initiated , current risk trajectory with alert rationale.”
We focused on developing a dynamic, explainable risk-prediction system tailored to mechanically ventilated ICU patients , Aimed to act as a silent, tireless companion that integrates dozens of variables simultaneously, detects subtle deterioration patterns, and provides interpretable alerts before overt clinical collapse.

Prediction window

At any time t, the model predicts whether the patient will die in the ICU within the next 24 hours a rolling, always-current risk estimate.

Stale-variable design

Every variable carries a Δt (time since last measurement). Older values are decayed or treated as less reliable, so a stale lab can’t silently anchor the risk estimate.

06 Objectives

Four objectives

  1. Dynamic mortality model. Design and implement a time-series ML model predicting 24-hour ICU mortality for mechanically ventilated patients.
  2. Stale-variable mechanism. Track time since last measurement per parameter and reduce the influence of outdated values.
  3. Interpretable alerts. Integrate SHAP and attention-weight visualization to surface the primary drivers of risk escalation.
  4. Working prototype. Demonstrate real-time risk monitoring and early-warning generation on public ICU datasets.
All objectives focus on time-series dynamics and explainability deliberately without pre-ICU comorbidity features.
